# Source editions for the rites (U002, U003, U005)

CANDIDATE list; see `README.md` in this directory. Nothing fetched; licences as
understood. The owner chose four traditions (U002: rabbinic Judaism, Anglican,
Roman Catholic in the older rite, Messianic Jewish) and the rites of U003:
Sabbath; Passover; the other appointed times, with Hanukkah and Purim; daily
Jewish prayer; the Supper; baptism; morning and evening prayer; marriage;
burial and mourning; the Christian year; anointing of the sick — and not
confession or absolution. Each entry in the book gives the liturgy's own words
and then how and why (U004 C). The “how and why” is authored here; the words
come from the editions below.

## Messianic Jewish practice

No public-domain liturgy of the modern Messianic movement was found: its
prayer books date from the 1970s onward and are under copyright. Proposed
instead, each to be confirmed by the owner:

| # | Source | What it supplies | Licence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | The rabbinic sources above | The order of each observance, which Messianic practice keeps | As above |
| M2 | The New Testament passages that show Jesus and the apostles keeping the appointed times | The readings a Messianic service adds | In the text |
| M3 | Authored explanation, under the two-model rule | How the two are joined in practice, said in our own words, with each claim tied to M1 or M2 | Ours |

This needs the owner: which community's practice is the reference, and whether
a living authority reads the authored account before it ships (round 003). The
Tree of Life Version stays outside the repository (I13).

## The Didache (U005 F)

Its instructions on baptism, fasting, the Lord's Prayer and the thanksgiving
over cup and bread are in the corpus (Lightfoot, cleared). The rite entries
cite it as the earliest witness to each.
