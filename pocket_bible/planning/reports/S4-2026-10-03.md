## What was found, in the order it matters

1. **The route.** Stage 2 found that drawing a page as an image costs 146–166
   ms on Windows. The toolkit has one place where a full-window picture is
   copied row by row instead of pixel by pixel: `draw_live_surface`. A
   surface drawn from `on_paint` does not have to be registered with the
   window, and unregistered it starts no clock. That is the route. Its cost
   for a 2120 × 1360 page on Shadow is 2.4 ms. The losers, same page, same
   machine, p50: `fill_image_pattern` 58 ms; nearest-sampled image 82 ms;
   RasterCanvas 90 ms; `draw_image` 137–143 ms in four variants. A surface
   that *is* registered presents in 2 ms too but keeps a 16 ms timer running
   forever (42.6 megacycles in ten idle seconds against 0.2–1.0), which the
   toolkit's own idle counters do not show.
2. **It is a behaviour, not a promise.** Nothing in the toolkit's documents
   says a control may draw an unregistered surface or that the copy path
   stays. Filed as M7; it is the one request the product now stands on.
3. **Launch got faster with a real page than it was with an empty one.**
   First frame on Shadow: 65 ms (was 211); on the Windows runner 30 ms (was
   104). The page no longer goes through `draw_image`.
4. **Composition is cheap enough that no page needs to be cached.** A spread
   draws in 1.1 ms (p50) at the default window on Shadow and 2.3 ms at the
   largest; laying out all three passages takes 1.3–5.9 ms. Only the spread
   on screen is held, as the owner asked for Windows (W304 A) — on every
   platform.
5. **The same pixels everywhere.** Layout is done in units of a millionth of
   the page height and every calculation that reaches a pixel is in whole
   numbers. The eight stored renderings match byte for byte on Windows
   (GCC 16), macOS arm64 (Clang 20) and Linux x64 and arm64 (GCC 13). Line
   and page breaks do not depend on the window.
6. **Ezra SIL holds; Noto Serif Hebrew does not.** Shaped, Ezra SIL sets
   6,335 of the 6,536 sequences with no fault; its real faults are 37
   sequences where two marks touch (0.08% of all occurrences, nearly all a
   holam meeting an accent) and 16 where a mark touches its letter. Noto
   Serif Hebrew prints the meteg on top of the vowel beside it in 702
   sequences, 4.8% of all occurrences: far worse than the three sequences
   the static test found. It stays rejected.
7. **Portsmouth's em dash is not a dash.** The face has no en dash and no
   dagger, as Stage 1 found; it also maps the em dash to a glyph 483
   thousandths of the em high (its hyphen is 63). The compositor draws all
   three, and makes small capitals by scaling capitals, as the owner directed
   (F302, F303 A). Question 2.
8. **The minimum window at scale 1 is small type.** The mockup's page is 3%
   of its width to the em; a spread in 600 × 420 gives a 9-pixel em.
   Question 1.
9. **macOS is still the tight platform**: 92.5 MiB resident with a spread
   open (77.3 MiB was the empty window), of the 150 MiB ceiling.

### How the page is made, in plain terms

Each choice below is mine where the owner's answers left room; none is a
decision.

- **Proportions and sizes** are the mockup's: a spread 1.43 times as wide as
  it is high; text area inset 6%, 9%, 7%, 9%; the em 3% of the page width
  (about 65 characters to the line, inside R023 C's 60–75); leading 1.32;
  small and large at 0.88 and 1.16; verse numerals at 0.62 of the em and
  raised; the drop numeral 2.9 ems over two lines; notes at 0.76; Hebrew at
  1.16 with leading 1.5; ink `#1d1812`, red `#a01c14`, paper `#f0ece0`.
- **Folding**: a window narrower than it is tall shows one page; the pages
  are the same pages, so the page breaks are too.
- **Line breaking** weighs the whole paragraph and takes the set of breaks
  with the least total looseness, so a line may end early where that packs
  the paragraph better (R006 B note). Without hyphenation a few lines come
  out with wide spaces: 0 to 10 in the whole slice depending on the face and
  size. The last line of a paragraph is set at its natural width.
- **Verse 1 under a drop numeral has no numeral of its own.**
- **Footnotes** are lettered a, b, c from the top of each page and set at the
  foot under a hairline. A line and its note never part.
- **Parallel-passage lines** (`\r`) are read and not printed: cross-references
  belong to a slip (R014 C).
- **Small capitals**: the BSB's LORD and GOD, after their first letter.
- **Study form** keeps the two pages in step by paragraph: a page takes a
  paragraph of English and its original only if both fit. This leaves white
  at the foot where the next paragraph is long (question 4).
- **Greek** shows the Nestle-Aland side only (B305 A): of 149 rows, 10 are
  not displayed, verse 16 among them, which the BSB carries in a note. The
  source's paragraph marks are read as structure, not printed.
- **Hebrew** is printed with its points, accents and section marks as the
  source has them; a psalm's title is a line of its own.
- **Word identity**, for clicks: an English word is its verse and its place
  in the verse; an original word is the source's own word number. Stage 3's
  token identity replaces this.

about 9 pixels (`assets/reference/reading-garamond-small-minimum.png`). On the
owner's display at scale 2 the same window is twice as sharp.
- A. As built: the spread always fits whole.
- B. In a small window show one page at the window's full width and let the
  wheel and keys move down it before turning.
- C. Raise the minimum window until the spread is readable (about 900 × 630).
Recommend **B**: "usable at 600 × 420" is the owner's rule (I20) and 9-pixel
type is not reading. It costs one more state in the view and no new pages.

**Q3. Portsmouth's em dash.**
The face's glyph for the em dash is not a dash (experiment 004), and the BSB
uses the em dash often. The compositor now draws it, as it draws the en dash
and the dagger.
- A. Keep the compositor's dash; leave the face as it is.
- B. The glyph is a mistake in the face; the owner will fix it, and the
  compositor's dash goes when a fixed build arrives.
Recommend **A**: it follows F302 exactly and needs nothing from anyone. B is
free to happen later; the compositor uses the face's own dash when it is one.

**Q4. The "fixed, measured correction" to linear-light blending (F009 B).**
None is applied: ink is blended over paper in linear light and looks a little
lighter than the browser mockup's text.
- A. None, as built.
- B. One darkening curve, chosen by the owner's eye at G2 from three samples,
  then fixed and recorded.
Recommend **B, at G2, only if the page looks light**: a correction has to be
measured against something, and here that is the owner's eye on this page.

**Q5. Study form: how the two pages keep step.**
As built a paragraph of English and its original go on a spread only if both
fit, so the foot of a page is sometimes empty (screenshot 2).
- A. As built: in step by paragraph.
- B. Fill each page; the two sides drift apart by up to a paragraph.
- C. In step by verse: tighter than A, and a paragraph may then split across
  spreads.
Recommend **A**: study form is for reading the two against each other, and A
is the only one where a verse's original is always on the facing page.

**Q6. Page breaks: made ahead, or on first open (J002 C)?**
Laying out the slice takes 1.3–5.9 ms; any machine computes the same breaks.
- A. On first open, a chapter at a time; nothing stored.
- B. A, and the container carries one page count per chapter per setting
  (about 14 kB) so pages can be numbered from the front of the book.
- C. Store every page break.
Recommend **B**: the breaks cost nothing to recompute and are identical
everywhere; only a page *number* needs what came before, and that is small.
