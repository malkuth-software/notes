Blasted metal glints

A blasted surface is a field of tiny facets with random tilts, and a pixel is bright when one of its facets faces the half-vector between light and eye. Many facets per pixel average out into the satin of bead blast; one or two per pixel break the sheen into the sparkle of sand blast. Each pixel is computed on its own, with no bitmap. Drag on the panel to move the light.

Against the photographs

The statistics are luminance on 0–255. Spread is the standard deviation within each band: the difference between the image blurred at 0, 1, 2 and 4 px. Skew measures the heavy tail of bright specks, which is what separates sparkle from noise. Noise symmetric about the mean, like the cloth-looking version, has a skew near 0; glints give a clearly positive skew. Each photograph also carries its camera's blur and JPEG/WebP compression, which soften the finest band, so match the 2–4 px band and the skew before the 1–2 px band.
