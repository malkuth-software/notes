Neon acrylic buttons

Illuminated push buttons: an acrylic cap over a neon tube in a dark well, raytraced per pixel on the CPU. Every light in the scene scales linearly with the tube's power, so each geometry is rendered once into two buffers: the unlit scene and the light from a tube at unit power. A frame is then tone(unlit + power × lit), which is one multiply-add per pixel. Blinking costs almost nothing, a steady button is drawn once, and only pressing changes the geometry. Click to toggle; the selected button can be edited on the left.

The scene is a rounded acrylic cap (n = 1.49, filleted edges) in a well cut into a black anodised plate, with a neon tube below the cap. Each pixel follows its ray: reflection from the cap (Fresnel, softbox and room), refraction in and out of the cap with total internal reflection, absorption in tinted acrylic (Beer–Lambert), scattering in frosted and opal acrylic, the glowing gas column and its halo, the well walls lit by the tube, light piped out at the polished edges, and light spilling and focusing onto the plate round the cap.
