Carried over from the engine renders

  1. Two materials, colour from the lamp. Clear acrylic (untinted) and opaque white plastic. OK is clear over a green lamp; CANCEL is white plastic over red lamps.
  2. The clear key has a matte black back. Unlit it is dark glass over a void: the cold wire is black on black, so no squiggle shows. A grey or glossy back silhouettes the wire.
  3. The coil is big and sparse, a few wide turns near the back, and its light diffuses far through a faint haze in the acrylic (the clear key stays see-through).
  4. White plastic reads white when off (lit by the room) and glows in the lamp's colour when on, from within: the light is scattered in the plastic, not painted on it.
  5. The surface has a texture, not the light. The top's normal carries a moulded relief (orange-peel waves from Stillwater's caustic surface, and a finer grain), so the room's reflection and the coil seen through the top are both broken up by it.
  6. Legend: black ink on the top, clean joins, antialiased in the key's own plane and seen in perspective; it blocks the light behind it.
  7. Gloss from a graded softbox: soft edges and a fade, so no hard band crosses the key.
  8. Nothing beyond the key's edge. The canvas is transparent outside the key; coverage is its alpha.
  9. Linear in lamp power. Each press position is traced once into an unlit and a lit buffer; a frame is tone(room × unlit + power × colour × lit), tone 1 − e−0.75 v. Press positions are traced in idle time and the press plays between them; a still key is not redrawn.
